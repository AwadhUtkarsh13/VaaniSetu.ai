## 1. Overview

### 1.1 What CPGRAMS is
CPGRAMS is an online platform, available 24x7, for citizens to lodge grievances with public authorities on any subject related to service delivery. It is administered by the Department of Administrative Reforms & Public Grievances (DARPG) and is connected to all Ministries and Departments of the Government of India and to State Governments.

### 1.2 Access channels
Grievances can be lodged on https://pgportal.gov.in, through the CPGRAMS mobile app, through the UMANG app, or with the help of a Common Service Centre.

## 2. Lodging a Grievance

### 2.1 Information required
A grievance should state the Ministry, Department or State Government concerned; the category or subject of the grievance; a clear description of the problem including relevant dates, places and reference numbers (such as a scheme registration number); and the citizen's contact details. Supporting documents may be attached in PDF format.

### 2.2 Registration number
On submission, a unique registration number is generated. The citizen receives it on the registered mobile number and e-mail and uses it to track the grievance.

### 2.3 Writing a good grievance
A grievance is resolved fastest when it addresses the correct authority, concerns a single issue, gives specific facts (what happened, when, where, which office or official), mentions any previous application or complaint number, and states the relief sought.

## 3. Matters Not Taken Up as Grievances

### 3.1 Excluded subjects
The following are not treated as grievances on CPGRAMS: subjudice matters or any matter concerning a judgment of a court; personal and family disputes; RTI matters; anything that affects the territorial integrity of the country or friendly relations with other countries; suggestions; and religious matters. Service matters of Government employees are taken up only after the internal channels have been exhausted.

## 4. Redressal Timelines

### 4.1 Time limit for redressal
As per DARPG guidelines of 2024, grievances should be redressed within a maximum of 21 days. Where more time is required, the concerned authority should give an interim reply to the citizen stating the expected timeline.

### 4.2 Tracking status
The status of a grievance can be tracked at any time on the CPGRAMS portal or app using the registration number.

## 5. Appeal and Feedback

### 5.1 Appeal
If a citizen is not satisfied with the resolution, an appeal can be filed against the closed grievance within 30 days, using the registration number. The appeal is decided by the Appellate Authority of the concerned Ministry or Department.

### 5.2 Feedback
After closure, citizens can rate the resolution on the portal, and may receive a feedback call. Poorly rated grievances are flagged for review.

## 6. Structuring a Grievance (VaaniSetu Guidance)

### 6.1 Routing to the correct department
Grievances about PM-KISAN payments, eKYC or registration should be addressed to the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare). Grievances about PM-JAY hospital treatment, Ayushman card or claims should be addressed to the National Health Authority (Ministry of Health and Family Welfare). Grievances about State-run services should be addressed to the concerned State Government.

### 6.2 Grievance categories
VaaniSetu classifies each grievance into one category: delay or non-receipt of benefit; wrongful rejection or exclusion; corruption or bribery demand; misconduct by an official; denial of service or treatment; error in records or documents; or other.

### 6.3 Urgency levels
High urgency applies to denial of emergency medical treatment, threats to life or safety, or loss of livelihood. Medium urgency applies to delayed benefits or payments and bribery demands. Low urgency applies to information requests, record corrections and procedural delays without immediate harm.
