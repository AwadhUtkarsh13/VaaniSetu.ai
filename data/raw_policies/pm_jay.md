## 1. Scheme Overview

### 1.1 Objective
Ayushman Bharat Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) is a government-funded health assurance scheme that provides financial protection against catastrophic health expenditure for secondary and tertiary care hospitalisation to poor and vulnerable families.

### 1.2 Launch and implementing agency
The scheme was launched on 23 September 2018 at Ranchi, Jharkhand. It is implemented by the National Health Authority (NHA) at the national level and by State Health Agencies (SHAs) in the States and UTs.

### 1.3 Funding pattern
PM-JAY is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme. Costs are shared between the Centre and States in the ratio 60:40, 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States, and 100% central funding for UTs without a legislature.

## 2. Benefits

### 2.1 Health cover
Each eligible family receives a health cover of up to ₹5 lakh per family per year on a family-floater basis for secondary and tertiary care hospitalisation.

### 2.2 Cashless and paperless treatment
Treatment is cashless and paperless at all empanelled public and private hospitals. Beneficiaries do not pay for services covered under the health benefit packages, and empanelled hospitals must not charge them for such services.

### 2.3 No restriction on family size, age or gender
There is no cap on family size, age or gender of members. All pre-existing conditions are covered from the first day of the policy.

### 2.4 Pre- and post-hospitalisation
The cover includes up to 3 days of pre-hospitalisation and 15 days of post-hospitalisation expenses, such as diagnostics and medicines.

### 2.5 Health benefit packages
The Health Benefit Package (HBP 2022) covers nearly 1,949 procedures across 27 specialities, including medical and surgical packages, oncology, cardiology, neonatal care and mental health, along with medicines, consumables, room charges, food and ICU charges.

### 2.6 National portability
A beneficiary can take treatment at any empanelled hospital anywhere in India, irrespective of the State in which the beneficiary is registered.

## 3. Eligibility

### 3.1 Entitlement-based scheme
PM-JAY is an entitlement-based scheme with no enrolment process or premium. Eligible families were identified from the deprivation and occupational criteria of the Socio-Economic Caste Census 2011 (SECC 2011) for rural and urban areas respectively, and the beneficiary base was revised in January 2022 to 12 crore families using additional data such as the National Food Security Act (NFSA) database.

### 3.2 Rural households — automatic inclusion
Rural households are automatically included if they are: households without shelter; destitute or living on alms; manual scavenger families; Primitive Tribal Groups; or legally released bonded labourers.

### 3.3 Rural households — deprivation criteria
Rural households are also eligible if they meet any of the following SECC deprivation criteria: D1 — only one room with kucha walls and kucha roof; D2 — no adult member between the ages of 16 and 59; D3 — female-headed household with no adult male member between 16 and 59; D4 — a disabled member and no able-bodied adult member; D5 — Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe household; D7 — landless household deriving the major part of its income from manual casual labour.

### 3.4 Urban households — occupational categories
Urban households are eligible if the main earning member belongs to one of 11 occupational categories: ragpicker; beggar; domestic worker; street vendor, cobbler, hawker or other service provider working on the street; construction worker, plumber, mason, labourer, painter, welder, security guard or coolie; sweeper, sanitation worker or gardener (mali); home-based worker, artisan, handicraft worker or tailor; transport worker such as driver, conductor, helper, cart puller or rickshaw puller; shop worker, assistant, peon in a small establishment, helper, delivery assistant, attendant or waiter; electrician, mechanic, assembler or repair worker; washerman or chowkidar.

### 3.5 Senior citizens aged 70 years and above
With effect from 29 October 2024, all citizens aged 70 years and above are eligible irrespective of their socio-economic status or income, and receive an Ayushman Vay Vandana card. Seniors belonging to families already covered under PM-JAY receive an additional top-up cover of up to ₹5 lakh per year for themselves, which is not shared with other family members. Seniors covered under other public health schemes such as CGHS, ECHS or CAPF may choose either their existing scheme or PM-JAY.

### 3.6 Frontline health workers
Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs), Anganwadi Workers and Anganwadi Helpers and their families are eligible for coverage under PM-JAY.

### 3.7 State expansions
Several States and UTs have expanded coverage beyond the central criteria using their own funds, often converging their State health schemes with PM-JAY. Eligibility under such expansions is State-specific and should be checked with the State Health Agency.

## 4. Exclusion Criteria

### 4.1 SECC automatic exclusion criteria (rural)
Rural households meeting any of the following criteria are automatically excluded from the SECC-based eligibility: owning a motorised two-, three- or four-wheeler or fishing boat; owning mechanised three- or four-wheeler agricultural equipment; holding a Kisan Credit Card with a credit limit of ₹50,000 or more; having any member employed by Government; running a non-agricultural enterprise registered with Government; having any member earning more than ₹10,000 per month; paying income tax; paying professional tax; living in a house with three or more rooms with pucca walls and roof; owning a refrigerator; owning a landline phone; owning 2.5 acres or more of irrigated land with at least one irrigation equipment; owning 5 acres or more of land irrigated for two or more crop seasons; owning 7.5 acres or more of land with at least one irrigation equipment.

### 4.2 Income tax payers and government employees
Households paying income tax and households with a member in regular Government employment are excluded under the SECC criteria; Government employees are generally covered by their own schemes such as CGHS. The exclusion does not apply to citizens aged 70 and above under Section 3.5.

### 4.3 Families not in the beneficiary database
A family that does not appear in the PM-JAY beneficiary database, and is not covered under the 70+ provision, the frontline-worker provision or a State expansion, is not eligible, even if it is economically poor. Such families may approach the State Health Agency regarding State-funded schemes.

## 5. How to Check Eligibility and Obtain the Ayushman Card

### 5.1 Checking eligibility
Eligibility can be checked on https://beneficiary.nha.gov.in, the Ayushman app, by calling the helpline 14555, or at any empanelled hospital or Common Service Centre, using a mobile number, ration card or Aadhaar number.

### 5.2 Ayushman card
Eligible beneficiaries complete Aadhaar-based eKYC to generate an Ayushman card. Generating the Ayushman card is free of cost. The card is not mandatory for treatment: an eligible beneficiary can be verified at the hospital with Aadhaar.

### 5.3 Documents
Beneficiaries typically need an Aadhaar card and, where applicable, a ration card or the PM letter / family identification document issued under the scheme.

## 6. Availing Treatment

### 6.1 Empanelled hospitals
Treatment is available at empanelled public and private hospitals, which can be searched on https://hospitals.pmjay.gov.in or in the Ayushman app.

### 6.2 Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitra
Each empanelled hospital has a Pradhan Mantri Arogya Mitra (PMAM) help desk that verifies beneficiaries, assists with admission and pre-authorisation, and guides the patient throughout treatment.

### 6.3 Denial of treatment or illegal charges
An empanelled hospital may not refuse treatment for a covered package or demand money from an eligible beneficiary for covered services. Any such denial or demand can be reported as a grievance under Section 7.

## 7. Grievance Redressal

### 7.1 Helpline
The toll-free PM-JAY helpline numbers are 14555 and 1800-11-4477.

### 7.2 Grievance portal and committees
Grievances can be filed on the Centralised Grievance Redressal Management System (CGRMS) at https://cgrms.pmjay.gov.in, or on CPGRAMS addressed to the National Health Authority / Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. A three-tier structure of District, State and National Grievance Redressal Committees, supported by grievance nodal officers, handles grievances.
